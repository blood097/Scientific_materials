1. `Don't give up your day job` 
<br>Kirichek, E.A., Tsyganova, A.V., Flores-Félix, J.D. *et al*. Symbiotic compatibility between *Rhizobium laguerreae* and its host. *Archives of Microbiology* 208, 91 (2026)
>The efficiency of interaction between legumes and rhizobia depends on the genotypes of both partners, which may lead to inefficient symbiosis. This study examined interactions between three pea (*Pisum sativum* L.) genotypes and six *Rhizobium laguerreae* strains classified into three genospecies (gsN, gsO, gsR) via whole-genome phylogenetic analysis. The peculiarities of interaction between each pea genotype and each strain were studied at histological and ultrastructural levels. Both normally developing symbioses and those with various disruptions in the infection process, bacterial release, differentiation of bacteria into bacteroids, and the ability of bacteroids to maintain their functional activity were identified. It was shown that cv. ‘Rondo’ was the most successful in forming symbioses with *R. laguerreae* strains, in turn, strain AMPS05 was the most effective on all three pea genotypes studied. Thus, the efficiency of interaction in symbioses formed between pea and *R. laguerreae* strains depends on the genotypes of both partners.
2. `Pan-genome_Bradyrhizobium`
<br>Kirichek, E.A., Tsyganov, V.E.
>Biological nitrogen fixation occupies a paramount position in sustainable agriculture. The present study elucidates the genomic diversity and symbiotic capabilities of *Bradyrhizobium ottawaense* focusing on the commercial *B. ottawaense* strain RCAM0104 to decipher the genetic bases of plant-bacterial interactions and the evolutionary adaptability of these microorganisms. Experimental assessments of symbiotic compatibility revealed that RCAM0104 effectively nodulates *Arachis hypogaea*, *Glycine soja*, and *Vigna unguiculata*, but is ineffective on *Glycine max*. Histological examinations confirmed successful infection processes in most host plants, with the exception of white nodules observed on cultivated soybean. Prediction of type III effectors in the RCAM0104 genome identified effector proteins potentially involved in its promiscuous symbiotic phenotype. These findings highlight the genetic flexibility of *B. ottawaense* and its capacity to adapt to diverse host plants. The observed host-specific nodulation patterns correlate with specific variations in the accessory genome, suggesting their potential role in determining symbiotic compatibility, potentially through gene transfer from local strains. This research contributes significantly to our understanding of the evolutionary dynamics and ecological plasticity of rhizobia in symbiosis with legumes, providing insights into the mechanisms regulating plant-microbe interactions. Based on these results, we hypothesize an evolutionary trade-off, where a loss of compatibility with one host may have occurred concurrently with the putative acquisition in compatibility with another.
